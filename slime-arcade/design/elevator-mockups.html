The Elevator

You are inside a station lift. Push a button, the doors close, the car moves, the doors open somewhere else — U‑Bahn in the basement, S‑Bahn in the main hall, shops above that, the ICE platform up top. Built from the same flat boxes, chunky ink outlines and brushed console as the train screen.

Indicator

Up and down arrows, the floor tile in that floor's own sign colour, and the floor name in Bungee. The moving arrow blinks — the same lamp behaviour the train's reverse notch already has.

Doorway

Two brushed leaves with rubber noses, each sliding out by its own width. Behind them, a column of four floor scenes that slides vertically. That translate is the entire animation — no path maths, unlike the train.

Panel

The train's brushed console turned portrait. Round buttons, because lift buttons are round and it separates them instantly from the train's square console. Order is top‑down 2 / 1 / E / U, the way a real panel reads.

Green ring

Where you are now. A pressed floor lights amber instead and holds that light for the whole ride, then goes out on arrival.

Sound row

The train screen's own row, six buttons: bell, doors opening, doors closing, next stop, mind the gap, welcome aboard. German first, English on the English setting.

Tap size

Every control clears 44 px at the smallest phone size; floor buttons land near 60. Nothing is ever disabled‑looking and nothing can fail.

One ride, start to finish

Pressing 2 from the main hall. Five states, about four and a half seconds end to end. This is a real sequence, so it is numbered.

Four floors of a composite German main station. Each scene is a single screen‑width slab of flat boxes — the same drawing grammar as the train cars, reusing their exact liveries: BVG yellow, S‑Bahn red and ochre, ICE white with the red cheat line.

Button states

Three states per control, all of them lit rather than greyed. Nothing on this screen ever looks broken or switched off — the arcade's rule, carried over intact.

Borrowed from the train screen

Almost nothing here is new machinery. The train screen already answered the hard questions; this one inherits the answers.

PieceWhere it comes fromWhat changes
ConsoleThe S‑Bahn driving console's gradient, border and inset highlightTurned portrait; buttons go round

AudioPre‑rendered, cached one‑shots — no live oscillatorsDing, hiss and beep are new parameter sets, not new code

LifecycleOne rAF loop started on entry, stopped on exit and on the parent timerSame hooks, one axis instead of a curved path

Deliberately out of scope: passengers to deliver, a score, a call panel on other floors, queued stops, an alarm phone. This is a machine to operate, not a puzzle to solve — a press during a ride just buzzes and is ignored, which is instant and honest rather than silently queued.
